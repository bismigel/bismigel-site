I did a stupid thing. I am writing it down, because the stupid thing had a useful lesson
inside it. That usually happens.

I tried to stream Jellyfin through my Cloudflare tunnel. Through the front door I built
so that nothing would ever be open directly. Through *Zero Trust*, with a login check on
it and everything.

It buffered. Of course it buffered. I just did not know why for a very long time.

* The tour of wrong answers

The video stuttered. So I did what I always do: I blamed the box.

** The server. Wrong.
Jellyfin converts video while you watch. So I assumed the little i5 could not keep up. I
checked the stats. It was converting at about **280 frames per second** — about ten times
real time — and it was a third of the way *ahead* of me. The server was bored. Not it.

** The disk. Also wrong.
The media storage is a mix of disks and a network share. I had trouble with it before. So
I checked. Reads came back at about 300 MB/s. Also bored.

** The throttle. Still wrong (but a fun detour).
Earlier, I had turned on a power-saving setting that pauses converting. Maybe it was
pausing and starving the player? I turned it off. It did not help.

** My internet. Very, very wrong.
Now the only suspect left was my connection. So I ran a speed test, ready to sigh.

#+begin_src shell
Ping     30.8 ms
Download 218 Mbit/s
Upload   450 Mbit/s
#+end_src

The *upload* was 450. Four hundred and fifty. My connection is a fire hose. Ten megabits
of video would not trouble it. So it was not my internet either. And now I had ruled out
everything except one thing.

* The real problem: the path

Here is the clue that broke it open. I looked at where the player's requests were *coming
from*. They came from a **pod's address** inside the cluster — the ingress. Not from my
laptop on the same network. So the stream was not going laptop -> server. It was going:

#+begin_example
laptop -> my ISP -> Cloudflare edge -> back down the tunnel -> ingress -> Jellyfin
#+end_example

A video from the same room, taking a trip through the internet and back. Because I had
opened the domain name instead of the IP address.

* And then the second, funnier problem

Fine, I thought. It is a detour, but a *fast* one. Then I read the part of Cloudflare's
rules that I had skipped. The free plan is explicitly not for "serving video or a
disproportionate percentage of ... large files". They do not give you a meter and point
at a number. They just do not want to be your media server. And they are allowed to make
that felt.

There is no switch that says "throttle this". There is no setting I missed. The
"throttle" *is* the rule. I went looking for a number to turn down, and I found a
paragraph instead.

And on top of that: every three-second piece of video had to pass through Cloudflare
**Access** — a full login check, for every piece, for a movie. I had built a bouncer for
a cinema.

* The fix, which took one second

I opened Jellyfin at its local address instead — =http://192.168.2.81:8096= — and it was
instant. No tunnel, no edge, no login check for every piece of video. Just a wire between
two machines that sit in the same room.

For watching *away* from home, the right answer is a VPN (I chose Tailscale). Then the
video goes straight to the device, and Cloudflare never sees a single frame of it. The
tunnel is for the *door*: the dashboards, the APIs, the login pages. It is not, and never
was, a fire hose.

* The lesson

I keep learning the same shape of thing. The tunnel is a great tool for exactly what it
is for. And I asked it to be something it is not. Then I spent an evening carefully
measuring every part of the house, except the one that was broken.

"Use the right path for the job" sounds so obvious that it is useless advice. Except I
had a 450-megabit upload and a server that was bored. And I still chose the slow way.
Because the slow way had a nice lock on it. The lock looked safe, so I held the door open
and waved it through.
