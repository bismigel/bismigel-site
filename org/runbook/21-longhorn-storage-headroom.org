#+TITLE: Longhorn has nowhere left to put a replica
#+SUMMARY: How to read the per-disk headroom Longhorn never exported, and the four steps that move replicas off the OS filesystem without losing a volume.
#+GROUP: Recovery

On 2026-10-30 =sitaw-amd= had *120 MiB* of room for the next replica on a disk
already holding 45 G of them. Every alert said the cluster was fine, because the
Longhorn alerts compare usage to capacity — and that disk was 43% "used".

Nothing was broken yet. What was broken is that the next rebuild on that node
would have failed, and that disk is =/var/lib/longhorn=, a directory on the
filesystem that carries etcd and the containerd image store. Filling it does not
degrade a volume. It takes the control plane down.

* The number that was missing

Longhorn admits a replica to a disk when *free space minus promised space* is
positive. It exports the scheduled bytes per /node/ only, so per disk the figure
was invisible; =longhorn_disk_usage_bytes= is what is on the disk now, not what is
promised to it.

The recording rule =longhorn:disk_scheduled_to_free_ratio= rebuilds it. It joins
=longhorn_replica_info= (which carries the disk path and the volume) with the
volume size, then divides by the free space of the filesystem that path lives on:

#+begin_src sh
curl -s -G 'http://127.0.0.1:30909/api/v1/query' \
  --data-urlencode 'query=longhorn:disk_scheduled_to_free_ratio' \
  | jq -r '.data.result[] | "\(.metric.node) \(.metric.disk_path) \(.value[1])"'
#+end_src

What it said on 2026-10-30, after the image and store cleanup in step 1:

| Node              | Disk path                 | Promised | Free on fs | Ratio |
|-------------------+---------------------------+----------+------------+-------|
| sitaw-amd         | =/var/lib/longhorn= (root fs) | 45 G | 53 G   | *0.84* |
| patola-thinkcentre | =/var/lib/longhorn= (root fs) | 55 G | 68 G  | *0.81* |
| patola-thinkcentre | =/mnt/data= (dedicated)   | 34 G     | 121 G      | 0.28  |
| sitaw-amd         | =/mnt/media-nvme= (dedicated) | 39 G  | 319 G      | 0.12  |
| calamansi-intel   | =/var/lib/longhorn= (root fs) | 5 G   | 58 G       | 0.09  |

The two 0.8 rows are the problem: /node/-level capacity looked comfortable (767 G
raw) while 105 G of the 178 G scheduled sat on operating-system disks.

* Which alert, what it means

| Alert | Sev | Means | Do |
|-------+-----+-------+----|
| =LonghornDiskSchedulingHeadroomLow= | warn | >75% of a disk's remaining free space is promised to replicas | this page, step 2 and 3 |
| =LonghornDiskSchedulingOverCommitted= | crit | >95%, no room for another replica of any size | step 2 *now*; a rebuild here will fail |
| =LonghornDataOnRootFilesystem= | warn | a =default-disk-*= holds >15% of the OS filesystem | step 4: it should not be there at all |
| =LonghornDiskStorageLow= | warn | actual usage >90% of capacity | step 1, then step 3 |
| =RootFilesystemDaysToFull= | warn | the root filesystem is projected to fill inside 7 days | step 1; check image store growth |

=LonghornDataOnRootFilesystem= firing is not an emergency. It is the invariant
that keeps the other four from mattering, and it stays red until step 4 is done.

* Step 1 — reclaim what is cheap first

Two things grow on the OS filesystems on their own. Reclaim them before moving any
data, because it changes how much you have to move:

#+begin_src sh
for h in 192.168.2.80 192.168.2.81 192.168.2.82; do
  ssh root@$h 'k3s crictl rmi --prune; nix-collect-garbage --delete-older-than 14d'
done
#+end_src

| Node | Before | After | Reclaimed |
|------+--------+-------+-----------|
| calamansi-intel | 58 G (62%) | 38 G (41%) | 20 G: 147 to 36 images, 61 generations |
| sitaw-amd | 53 G (57%) | 40 G (43%) | 13 G: 161 to 42 images, 74 generations |
| patola-thinkcentre | 33 G (36%) | 25 G (27%) | 8 G: 49 to 36 images |

kubelet only garbage-collects images at 85% disk usage, so dead images are parked
on the node indefinitely. That is 13-16 G per node here, replaced by nothing.

This bought =sitaw-amd= 13 G of headroom instead of 120 MiB. It did not fix the
shape of the problem: Longhorn is still storing replicas on the OS filesystem.

* Step 2 — stop new placements

Turn off scheduling on the disk that is out of room. Nothing moves; the point is
that nothing else lands there while you work.

#+begin_src sh
kubectl -n longhorn-system patch nodes.longhorn.io sitaw-amd --type=merge \
  -p '{"spec":{"disks":{"default-disk-18b0d424735c5693":{"allowScheduling":false}}}}'
kubectl -n longhorn-system patch nodes.longhorn.io patola-thinkcentre --type=merge \
  -p '{"spec":{"disks":{"default-disk-6aef81d7852e3ab6":{"allowScheduling":false}}}}'
#+end_src

*Do not guess the disk IDs.* Scheduling off on the wrong one takes a dedicated
disk out of the pool. Read them first:

#+begin_src sh
kubectl -n longhorn-system get nodes.longhorn.io <node> -o json \
  | jq -r '.spec.disks | to_entries[] | "\(.key) \(.value.path)"'
#+end_src

| Node | Disk name | Path | UUID |
|------+-----------+------+------|
| sitaw-amd | default-disk-18b0d424735c5693 | =/var/lib/longhorn= | =1a6514e3-ea70-4d84-b531-102d06c32b9c= |
| sitaw-amd | nvme | =/mnt/media-nvme= | =4df00a93-8f0e-45a3-9298-0c37987c523e= |
| patola-thinkcentre | default-disk-6aef81d7852e3ab6 | =/var/lib/longhorn= | =c1229a26-3b81-418f-adc5-0929fdba8abd= |
| patola-thinkcentre | data | =/mnt/data= | =bc806371-0286-44f1-aa1e-01c5ce9e671d= |
| calamansi-intel | default-disk-9fc632c1dea049fc | =/var/lib/longhorn= | =b353dec7-9be0-48a2-a6e6-0710b30c6d2b= |

Leave =calamansi-intel= alone if it has no dedicated disk: with every root disk
unschedulable it has nowhere to receive a replica, and it is the fallback when one
of the other two nodes dies.

* Step 3 — evacuate, one replica at a time

=Scheduling off= only stops new placements. Existing replicas have to be deleted so
Longhorn rebuilds them elsewhere. The rebuild scheduler prefers the disk with the
most free space and keeps away from the node already holding another replica of
that volume, so in practice the replicas land on the dedicated disks without any
selector work.

Drop the rebuild throttle first. It is 600 s between rebuilds and there are about
twenty to do:

#+begin_src sh
kubectl -n longhorn-system patch settings replica-replenishment-wait-interval \
  --type=merge -p '{"value":"120"}'
#+end_src

Then delete replicas by disk UUID, with a health gate before each one:

#+begin_src sh
for disk in 1a6514e3-ea70-4d84-b531-102d06c32b9c \
            c1229a26-3b81-418f-adc5-0929fdba8abd; do
  for r in $(kubectl -n longhorn-system get replicas.longhorn.io -o json \
      | jq -r --arg d "$disk" '.items[] | select(.spec.diskID==$d) | .metadata.name'); do
    vol="${r%-r-*}"
    [ "$(kubectl -n longhorn-system get volumes.longhorn.io "$vol" \
        -o jsonpath='{.status.robustness}')" = healthy ] \
      || { echo "SKIP $r (volume not healthy)"; continue; }
    echo ">> $r"
    kubectl -n longhorn-system delete replicas.longhorn.io "$r"
    until [ "$(kubectl -n longhorn-system get volumes.longhorn.io "$vol" \
        -o jsonpath='{.status.robustness}')" = healthy ]; do sleep 15; done
  done
done
kubectl -n longhorn-system patch settings replica-replenishment-wait-interval \
  --type=merge -p '{"value":"600"}'
#+end_src

*The health gate is not decoration.* Every volume here runs 2 replicas. If a
volume is already degraded and you delete its remaining replica, there is nothing
to rebuild from and the volume is gone. The loop skips those; run it again later
instead of forcing it.

Expect 100 G of rebuild traffic. The 30 G volume is the long one, about five
minutes at 1 GbE. Watch it with:

#+begin_src sh
kubectl -n longhorn-system get volumes.longhorn.io \
  -o custom-columns=NAME:.metadata.name,SIZE:.spec.size,ROBUST:.status.robustness \
  --no-headers | grep -v healthy
#+end_src

* Step 4 — keep it off the root filesystem

Evacuating once is a cleanup. The rule is what stops it coming back, and the rule
is *not in git*: Longhorn disk registration, tags and =allowScheduling= live only in
the =nodes.longhorn.io= custom resource. A disko reinstall registers a fresh
=default-disk-*= with scheduling on, and nothing notices. That is documented drift,
not a bug to file.

So: leave scheduling off on the root-fs disks, and for anything that must stay
dynamic (a disk added later, a node rebuilt), tag the dedicated disks and let a
StorageClass do the filtering.

| Disk | Tag |
|------+-----|
| =patola-thinkcentre/data= | =dedicated= |
| =sitaw-amd/nvme= | =dedicated= |

#+begin_src yaml
# bakuran-gitops, alongside longhorn-config. The `longhorn` class is Helm-owned,
# so declare a sibling instead of patching it.
apiVersion: storage.k8s.io/v1
kind: StorageClass
metadata: { name: longhorn-dedicated }
provisioner: driver.longhorn.io
allowVolumeExpansion: true
reclaimPolicy: Delete
volumeBindingMode: Immediate
parameters:
  numberOfReplicas: "2"
  diskSelector: "dedicated"
  staleReplicaTimeout: "30"
  fsType: "ext4"
  dataEngine: "v1"
#+end_src

* Verify

Two things, and both should be boring:

#+begin_src sh
# 1. how much Longhorn still holds on each root-fs disk (all should be 0)
curl -s -G 'http://127.0.0.1:30909/api/v1/query' \
  --data-urlencode 'query=longhorn_disk_usage_bytes{disk=~"default-disk-.*"}' \
  | jq -r '.data.result[] | "\(.metric.node) \(.value[1])"'

# 2. every volume healthy, nothing rebuilding
kubectl -n longhorn-system get volumes.longhorn.io \
  -o custom-columns=NAME:.metadata.name,ROBUST:.status.robustness --no-headers \
  | grep -cv healthy
#+end_src

=0= from the second command, and a =0= for each node in the first, is the end
state. =LonghornDataOnRootFilesystem= clears an hour after the last replica leaves.

* Traps

- *Scheduling off frees nothing.* It is a brake, not a mop. Set it and walk away
  and the disk is still full.
- *Never delete a replica of a degraded volume.* 2 replicas, no exception.
- *=kubectl scale= on a media app is undone by ArgoCD self-heal in seconds.*
  Anything holding =/mnt/media= open has to be scaled down in git first, or the
  remount fails (see the 2026-10-28 entry in =KNOWN-ISSUES.md=).
- *Raising =storage-minimal-available-percentage= is not a substitute.* It makes
  Longhorn refuse more placements; it does not move the ones already there.
- *The ratio also rises when containerd fills the root filesystem*, because the
  denominator shrinks. If it climbs without new volumes, step 1 is the answer.

* What it bought

178 G scheduled, 105 G of it on the filesystems that run the control plane. After
step 1 the next replica had 13 G to land in instead of 120 MiB. After step 3 it
has no business being there at all. The alerts that say so are new, and they were
written from the number this outage was missing rather than the one that was
already on the dashboard.
